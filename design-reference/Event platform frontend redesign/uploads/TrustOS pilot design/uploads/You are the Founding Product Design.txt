You are the Founding Product Designer and Creative Director for TrustOS.

Use the official Anthropic frontend-design skill if it is available.

You are not creating a cosmetic redesign.

You are defining how TrustOS should feel, behave and communicate during its
first real MiniMomentz pilot.

Read all supplied materials before designing:

1. TrustOS Product Handbook
2. Existing public website
3. Current application screenshots
4. Current vendor workflow
5. Current client workflow
6. Venue research page
7. Existing brand colours and product principles

Do not blindly preserve the current app interface.

Preserve the working product logic, but redesign the experience from the
perspective of a real human using it for an actual project.

==================================================
FOUNDER INTENT
==================================================

This product comes from a real frustration.

After a client pays a deposit, they often experience silence and uncertainty.

The vendor is also managing:

- client details;
- WhatsApp messages;
- dates;
- payments;
- mood boards;
- preparation;
- location details;
- deadlines;
- delivery;
- reviews;

across too many disconnected places.

TrustOS should make both sides feel:

- calm;
- informed;
- professionally supported;
- clear about what has happened;
- clear about what happens next.

The founder is not currently testing how much money this can make.

The founder is testing whether this genuinely improves the way MiniMomentz
runs a client project.

This is a learning pilot.

The design must feel sincere, thoughtful and useful — not like a venture-funded
dashboard pretending to have hundreds of customers.

==================================================
CORE PRODUCT PROMISE
==================================================

One workspace for the whole job.

From the first project setup to final delivery.

The vendor should always understand:

1. What has happened?
2. What is waiting?
3. What should I do next?

The client should always understand:

1. Where is my project?
2. Is anything required from me?
3. What happens next?

==================================================
THREE EXPERIENCES
==================================================

Design one coherent system containing:

1. TrustOS Admin Console
2. Vendor Workspace
3. Client Portal

They share one product language and design system, but each must feel suited
to its role.

Do not design three unrelated products.

==================================================
1. TRUSTOS ADMIN CONSOLE
==================================================

Audience:

The founder operating the TrustOS pilot.

Purpose:

Monitor whether vendors and projects are working correctly.

This is not a business-analytics dashboard.

Show only:

- workspaces;
- active vendors;
- active projects;
- projects waiting on vendor;
- projects waiting on client;
- recent signups;
- failed or blocked projects;
- system warnings;
- research submissions.

The design should feel:

- quiet;
- operational;
- trustworthy;
- compact;
- easy to scan.

Do not expose private client messages or sensitive payment details in the
overview.

==================================================
2. VENDOR WORKSPACE
==================================================

Audience:

A photographer, livestream operator, DJ, makeup artist, decorator or event
professional.

Many users may be 45–60 years old and may have little experience with business
software.

They should need no training.

The Vendor Workspace must use one shared design system.

Different professions should adapt through:

- terminology;
- project type;
- Event Details;
- preparation checklist;
- delivery requirements;

not through completely separate dashboard designs.

Primary navigation:

- Today
- Projects
- Clients
- Settings

Do not show analytics or templates prominently during the pilot.

==================================================
TODAY
==================================================

Today is not a dashboard of statistics.

Today is the vendor’s working starting point.

The most important feature is:

DO THIS FIRST

This is the single highest-priority action across every active project.

Examples:

- Review Chaitanya’s Event Details
- Confirm tomorrow’s venue
- Record the cash deposit
- Add the Rao Family delivery link

The Do This First component must be visually unforgettable.

It should show:

- project/client;
- clear action;
- why it matters;
- urgency;
- one dominant button.

Below it show only:

- Today’s shoots or services
- Waiting on me
- Waiting on client
- Upcoming deadlines
- New Project

Do not show vanity charts or generic statistics.

==================================================
PROJECT WORKSPACE
==================================================

Inside every project, the strongest feature is:

ONE CLEAR NEXT ACTION

This component must never visually blend into normal cards.

It should show:

- current stage;
- who is responsible;
- one plain-language action;
- one primary CTA;
- minimal supporting actions.

Examples:

“Review and send the quote”
“Chaitanya needs to confirm the Event Details”
“No payment required — begin preparation”
“Add the final delivery link”

The project journey is:

1. Project created
2. Client link sent
3. Event Details completed
4. Quote accepted
5. Payment confirmed or not required
6. Preparation
7. Service completed
8. Delivery sent
9. Client approved
10. Archived

Completed stages should feel reassuring.

The current stage should feel obvious.

Future stages should stay quiet.

Do not show all stages and tools with equal visual importance.

Supporting sections:

- Overview
- Client
- Money
- Preparation
- Delivery
- Messages

History should remain collapsed.

==================================================
3. CLIENT PORTAL
==================================================

The client does not need a dashboard.

The client receives one secure link.

No account.
No app download.
No vendor navigation.
No internal clutter.

The client sees:

- vendor/business name;
- project name;
- project progress;
- one current action;
- Event Details;
- quote;
- payment status;
- messages;
- delivery link.

The client experience should feel closer to a guided concierge page than
business software.

They must understand what to do within five seconds.

==================================================
EVENT DETAILS
==================================================

Do not use “Questionnaire” as the dominant customer-facing word.

Use:

- Event Details
- Project Details
- Confirm your details

The client is not making a new sales enquiry.

The vendor has already created the project.

Therefore remove questions such as:

- What is your budget?
- What package do you want?
- What price do you expect?

Only ask practical information needed to complete the work.

Forms should change according to project type.

Photography examples:

- participants and ages;
- location;
- accessibility;
- visual preferences;
- important photographs;
- clothing or mood-board preferences.

Live-stream examples:

- venue contact;
- timings;
- internet availability;
- streaming platform;
- audio sources;
- load-in time;
- power restrictions.

Completed Event Details should collapse into a concise summary.

==================================================
PAYMENTS
==================================================

Design three clear modes:

1. Pay securely online
2. Pay manually
3. No payment required — collaboration

Do not show technical Stripe errors or configuration language.

The vendor defines the amount and payment method.

The client only reviews and completes the required action.

Manual payment should clearly show:

- instructions;
- client confirmation;
- vendor confirmation.

==================================================
VISUAL EMOTION
==================================================

The product should feel:

- calm;
- warm;
- premium;
- human;
- professional;
- creatively intelligent;
- reassuring;
- quietly confident.

It should not feel:

- cold;
- corporate;
- childish;
- over-designed;
- generic;
- like an AI-generated SaaS template;
- like a marketing page converted into a dashboard.

The founder works in photography and visual storytelling.

The design should reflect genuine creative taste without sacrificing usability.

==================================================
AESTHETIC DIRECTION
==================================================

Choose one strong aesthetic direction:

“Warm editorial utility.”

This means:

- refined editorial character;
- practical application structure;
- calm paper-like surfaces;
- confident forest-green brand colour;
- distinctive but readable typography;
- subtle visual depth;
- controlled information density;
- human microcopy;
- restrained motion.

Use the public website as a brand reference, not as an app layout.

Do not put marketing hero sections inside the application.

Do not use huge headings inside daily work screens.

Do not use generic purple gradients.

Do not use random illustrations.

Do not use excessive glassmorphism.

Do not cover every screen in oversized cards.

Avoid default-looking Inter/Space Grotesk SaaS combinations unless there is a
deliberate reason.

Choose a more distinctive but highly readable display and interface type
pairing.

==================================================
USABILITY
==================================================

Design for a non-technical 45–60-year-old vendor.

Requirements:

- large touch targets;
- high contrast;
- plain language;
- obvious Back navigation;
- one dominant action;
- clear success confirmation;
- field-level validation;
- useful empty states;
- no hidden essential actions;
- no icon-only critical actions;
- phone-first layouts;
- readable text;
- no horizontal scrolling;
- minimal memory burden.

Never make the user remember where a feature lives.

Never show an empty page without explaining the next action.

Not robotic.
Not cute.

==================================================
WORKING METHOD
==================================================

Before designing screens:

1. Audit the supplied app screenshots.
2. Identify what makes them feel generic or AI-generated.
3. Identify confusing navigation.
4. Identify competing calls to action.
5. Identify wording that exposes internal implementation.
6. Identify what should be removed, not merely restyled.
7. Define the chosen aesthetic direction.
8. Explain the emotional logic behind the direction.

Then design.

Do not simply recolour the existing screens.

Do not create visual concepts without interaction logic.

Every screen must demonstrate:

- user;
- purpose;
- state;
- primary action;
- next state.

==================================================
FINAL STANDARD
==================================================

Ask these questions before finishing:

Would Ravi trust this with a real MiniMomentz project tonight?

Would his client understand the link without explanation?

Would a 50-year-old photographer know what to do next?

Does this feel designed by someone who understands creative professionals?

Is “Do This First” unmistakable?

Is “One Clear Next Action” unmistakable?

Does the product feel calm rather than empty?

Does it feel premium rather than decorative?

Does it reflect a founder who genuinely cares about the working experience?

If any answer is no, refine it before presenting the result.
